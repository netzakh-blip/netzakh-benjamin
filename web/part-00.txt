<p>Charleston journalist Isaac Harby defined Jewish citizenship before he organized religious reform.  In an 1816 protest against the removal of Jewish diplomat Mordecai Manuel Noah, Harby insisted that Jews held office by “equal inalienable, constitutional Rights” because they formed “a portion of the people”; nine years later, he called the United States a Jewish “Land of promise.”<sup class="fn-ref" id="fnref-1"><a aria-label="Footnote 1" href="#fn-1">1</a></sup>  Harby’s audience at the Reformed Society of Israelites included the first organized Jewish reformers in the United States, who sought English prayers, comprehensible sermons, shorter services, individual judgment, and a Judaism fitted to republican citizenship.
